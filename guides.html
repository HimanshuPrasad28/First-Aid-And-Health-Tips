Detailed step-by-step procedures for various emergency situations

Cardiac Emergencies

CPR for Adults

  1. Check the scene for safety and the person for responsiveness.
  2. Call 911 or ask someone else to do so.
  3. Place the person on their back on a firm, flat surface.
  4. Kneel beside the person's neck and shoulders.
  5. Place the heel of your hand on the center of the person's chest.
  6. Place your other hand on top of the first hand. Keep your elbows straight and position your shoulders directly above your hands.
  7. Use your upper body weight to push straight down on the chest at least 2 inches but not greater than 2.4 inches. Push hard at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute.
  8. If you're trained in CPR, you can now open the airway and give rescue breaths. If not, continue chest compressions.
  9. Continue CPR until there are signs of movement or emergency medical personnel take over.

CPR for Children

  1. Check the scene for safety and the child for responsiveness.
  2. If the child doesn't respond, call 911 or ask someone else to do so.
  3. Place the child on their back on a firm, flat surface.
  4. Open the airway by tilting the head back slightly and lifting the chin.
  5. Check for breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
  6. If the child isn't breathing, give 2 rescue breaths (if trained).
  7. Begin chest compressions: Place the heel of one hand on the center of the child's chest.
  8. Push straight down on the chest about 2 inches. Push at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute.
  9. Continue CPR until there are signs of movement or emergency medical personnel take over.

Using an AED

  1. Turn on the AED and follow the visual and/or audio prompts.
  2. Open the person's shirt and wipe their chest dry if needed.
  3. Attach the AED pads to the person's bare chest.
  4. Make sure no one, including you, is touching the person.
  5. Push the "Analyze" button if instructed by the AED.
  6. If the AED advises a shock, make sure no one is touching the person and press the "Shock" button.
  7. Begin CPR after the shock or if no shock is advised.
  8. Continue to follow the AED prompts until help arrives.

Choking & Breathing

Heimlich Maneuver for Adults

  1. Stand behind the person and wrap your arms around their waist.
  2. Make a fist with one hand and place it slightly above the person's navel.
  3. Grab your fist with your other hand.
  4. Press hard into the abdomen with a quick, upward thrust.
  5. Repeat thrusts until the object is expelled or the person becomes unconscious.

Heimlich Maneuver for Children

  1. Stand behind the child and wrap your arms around their waist.
  2. Make a fist with one hand and place it slightly above the child's navel.
  3. Grab your fist with your other hand.
  4. Press hard into the abdomen with a quick, upward thrust.
  5. Repeat thrusts until the object is expelled or the child becomes unconscious.

Recovery Position

  1. Kneel beside the person.
  2. Place the arm nearest to you at a right angle to their body, elbow bent with the palm facing up.
  3. Bring their other arm across their chest and hold the back of their hand against their cheek nearest to you.
  4. With your other hand, bend their far knee up at a right angle.
  5. Carefully roll them onto their side by pulling on the bent knee.
  6. Adjust the upper leg so both the hip and knee are bent at right angles.
  7. Tilt their head back to keep the airway open.
  8. Monitor their breathing and pulse until help arrives.

Bleeding & Wounds

Controlling Severe Bleeding

  1. Apply direct pressure on the wound using a clean cloth, bandage, or your hand if nothing else is available.
  2. If possible, raise the wounded area above the level of the heart.
  3. If blood soaks through, add more material without removing the original dressing.
  4. Use pressure points if direct pressure doesn't control bleeding.
  5. Apply a tourniquet only as a last resort for life-threatening bleeding that cannot be controlled by other means.
  6. Secure the wound with a bandage once bleeding is controlled.
  7. Seek immediate medical attention.

Treating Minor Wounds

  1. Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water.
  2. Clean the wound with mild soap and running water.
  3. Remove any dirt or debris with tweezers cleaned with alcohol.
  4. Apply an antibiotic ointment to prevent infection.
  5. Cover the wound with a sterile bandage.
  6. Change the bandage daily or whenever it gets wet or dirty.
  7. Watch for signs of infection (increased pain, redness, swelling, warmth, or drainage).

Treating Burns

  1. For minor burns, cool the burn with cool (not cold) running water for 10-15 minutes.
  2. Remove rings or other tight items from the burned area.
  3. Don't break blisters.
  4. Apply lotion with aloe vera.
  5. Bandage the burn loosely with a sterile gauze bandage.
  6. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever if needed.
  7. For severe burns, call 911 immediately. Cover the area with a cool, moist bandage or clean cloth.
